# Micromegas (MM) HV operating-point maps

Drift / resist HV guidance for the four Micromegas tracker detectors (A, B, C,
D), derived from beam HV scans. Unlike the plastic/wall folders (which hold
*energy/threshold* calibrations), this folder holds **HV operating points and
recommended scan ranges** — where to run each chamber, and where to look next.

## Files

- `statistics_run_config_run67.json` — **ready-to-set operating point for a
  STATISTICS run** (most reconstructed tracks per spill across the whole
  1–76 ms gate). Generated by
  `nTof_x17/ntof_july_analysis/run67_scan/export_stats_config.py`.
- `resist_hv_run67.json` — **per-detector resist-HV fine-scan ranges** from
  run_67 (2026-07-22/23), for a follow-up scan that zooms in on each chamber's
  best resist. Generated by
  `nTof_x17/ntof_july_analysis/run67_scan/resist_zoom.py` — regenerate, do not
  hand-edit. Carries the measured `P(3D pair)` and `blind_frac` vs resist
  profiles it is derived from, so a downstream reader can see the evidence, not
  just the conclusion.

### These two files disagree on purpose — read this before using either

|  | `statistics_run_config_run67.json` | `resist_hv_run67.json` |
|---|---|---|
| goal | most tracks per spill, **whole gate** | best tracking in the **2–8 ms IPC window** |
| gives | a setpoint to run at | a range to *scan* next |
| resist | higher (A 540, C 525, D 520) | lower (A 515–535, C 515–535, D 510–530) |
| reco | post-reprocessing (2026-07-25/26) | **pre**-reprocessing (2026-07-23) |

The resist optimum **moves with time since the gamma flash** — low gain early,
high gain late (evidence in `resist_optimum_by_window` in the statistics JSON).
So a whole-gate setting is necessarily higher than an IPC-window setting. Use
the statistics file when you want tracks; use the resist-zoom file when you are
planning an IPC-signal scan.

> **Caveat on `resist_hv_run67.json` (2026-07-26):** it was generated before the
> 2026-07-24 small-pulse reprocessing, so its absolute efficiencies are ~10×
> low (it quotes `p_pair` ≈ 0.010 where the re-reco'd data gives ≈ 0.11). The
> *shapes* it is built on still hold and its recommended ranges remain sensible,
> but regenerate it (`resist_zoom.py`) before quoting any number from it.

## Operational quick answer: "set up a statistics run"

Read `statistics_run_config_run67.json`. Set **drift 700 V on all four**
(operator choice 2026-07-26; also raises drift speed), the plastic
discriminator to **0.90 MIP**, and resist per detector:

| Det | drift | resist | 2σ band | expected P(3D pair)/trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 700 V | **540 V** | 535–540 | 0.115 ± 0.002 |
| **B** | 700 V | **540 V** | 540–545 | 0.100 ± 0.002 |
| **C** | 700 V | **525 V** | 525–535 | 0.134 ± 0.002 |
| **D** | 700 V | **520 V** | 520–530 | 0.097 ± 0.002 |

Anywhere inside the 2σ band is statistically the same run — do not chase the
peak. C and D want ~15 V less than A and B; that is a real per-chamber gain
difference, so set them individually rather than on a common setpoint.

**Plastic threshold 0.90 MIP** is chosen on *throughput* (tracks banked per
spill), not per-trigger efficiency. Throughput rises toward lower threshold on
all four chambers (Det A 995 → 1459 tracks/cell going 1.41 → 0.90 MIP); on A and
D the per-trigger efficiency rises too, so the extra triggers a lower threshold
records are *better* than average, not junk.

### Three caveats that matter operationally

1. **Efficiency only.** run_67 carries no spark or stability information. If a
   listed resist sits near a chamber's discharge threshold, that overrides this
   file.
2. **Det B works only at drift 700 V** — it is 10–30× better than at 500/600 V
   at *every* resist point, i.e. essentially dead below 700. That is a hardware
   symptom (drift-field delivery or gap), not a setting to design around; worth
   chasing before trusting B's numbers.
3. **Several optima sit at the edge of what was scanned**: threshold 0.90 MIP
   (bottom of the ladder), drift 700 V (top), Det D resist 520 V (bottom). The
   true optima may lie outside the scan — extending those three edges is worth
   more than refining the interior.

## Operational quick answer: "what resist range do I scan per detector?"

Fixed **drift 600–700 V** (operator choice, 2026-07-23). Read
`resist_hv_run67.json → detectors.<A|B|C|D>.fine_scan_resist_V.range`:

| Det | role | fine-scan resist | optimum | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | early | **515–535 V** | 525 V | clean M1 reference; alive to 535, saturates above 540 |
| **B** | early | **535–555 V** | 545 V | noisy M1, never saturates; weak gain-driven peak |
| **C** | early | **515–535 V** | 525 V | best early tracker; saturates *sharply* above 535 |
| **D** | **late** | **510–530 V** | 520 V (20–30 ms) | dead at 2–8 ms — see below |

Suggested step ~2 V (≈10 points per range).

## The principle (so a scan-builder can extrapolate)

Target = being **alive in the 2–8 ms** post-flash window (that is where the IPC
signal lives; it is gone by ~15 ms). The early-window resist optimum is the
**highest resist that is still alive**: raising resist raises gain (→ tracking
efficiency) but also lengthens post-flash saturation (→ blindness), and the two
cross at the saturation knee. So these early-window ranges sit **deliberately
below** a late-optimised setting — late windows (≥20 ms) keep preferring higher
resist, still rising at the 550 V ladder top.

## Det D is a late (≥20 ms) detector — do NOT optimise it for 2–8 ms

D is **98 % blind at 2–4 ms and produces zero tracks until ~12 ms**, at every
resist in 520–550. It only tracks well from ~20 ms on (where it is in fact the
best chamber, P up to ~26/1000). Decision recorded by the operator
(2026-07-23): **treat D as a late detector.** The range above targets its
20–30 ms window; if the later tail matters, D keeps improving toward 540–545 V
in 30–50 ms (raise to 535–555 V). A `fine_scan_resist_V.alt_for_early_aliveness`
(500–520 V) is recorded only as the alternative if early D aliveness is ever
chased — it trades gain for faster recovery and is *not* the chosen path.

`export_stats_config.py` deliberately uses **non-overlapping** dt windows. Do
not derive operating points by pooling `slide/slide_curves.csv`: that table is
a *boxcar* (6 ms wide, 1 ms step), so its rows overlap by ~83 % and summing them
counts each event ~6× and shrinks the errors by ~√6.

Pooled over drift {600,700} V and all three plastic thresholds
(0.90 + 1.13 + 1.41 MIP; HV response is threshold-independent). Efficiency is
RELATIVE (single-arm events dominate; no absolute normalization). Full method +
caveats in the JSON `provenance` block.
